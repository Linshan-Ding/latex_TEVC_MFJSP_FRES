\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The multiplicity flexible job-shop scheduling problem (MFJSP) describes a shop in which only a few job types are
produced, each type in many copies that share one routing, one candidate machine set per operation and one processing
time per machine. Electronics assembly, injection moulding and batch machining shops take this form: a planning window
holds thousands of operations but only a dozen products. \pending{A planner issues one executable schedule per window;
with job-indexed genetic search the solution quality degrades visibly as the number of copies doubles.} The difficulty is
structural rather than one of size: interchangeable copies create many equivalent solutions under a job-indexed
representation, and the loss incurred when continuous quotas are rounded to integers has a different origin from the loss
incurred by sequencing under fixed quotas.

\pending{Fluid relaxations of the MFJSP give meaningful flow ratios and lower bounds, and fluid-assisted evolutionary and
reinforcement-learning methods have appeared steadily in recent years.} These methods treat the fluid solution as an
initialization, a state feature or a control signal. They do not say which part of the discrete difficulty the fluid model
already explains, so the learning component faces the whole improvement problem and has no non-learned candidates to fall
back on when it fails out of distribution.

This paper starts from an exact identity. The gap between the fluid lower bound and a feasible makespan is the sum of a
rounding loss, which depends only on the integer quotas, and a sequencing residual, which depends on the order chosen
under those quotas. The identity suggests a division of labour: an evolutionary search over a quota chromosome whose
dimension is fixed by the type structure, a ranker that learns only the sequencing residual, and exact decoding as the sole
arbiter of acceptance.

The contributions are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A quota-compressed representation and its fluid--quota geometry (Section~\ref{sec:quota}): the genotype dimension does
  not grow with the number of copies, quota transfers conserve the per-type totals, and the rounding loss of the
  largest-remainder quota is bounded independently of the multiplicity (Proposition~\ref{prop:rounding}).
  \item A residual-scoped quotient ranker (Section~\ref{sec:ranker}) that predicts only the sequencing residual, with labels
  normalized by the fluid bound and an output that never undercuts the analytic lower bound.
  \item A safeguarded preselection (Section~\ref{sec:safeguard}) uniting learned, fluid-heuristic and random candidates under
  exact decoding, which is locally no worse than the heuristic alone (Proposition~\ref{prop:safeguard}) and is judged by a
  falsifiable decoding-count threshold.
  \item \pending{An experimental study on 59 fixed design instances, the Brandimarte benchmark and a multiplicity-extrapolation
  tier, with factor-effect analysis of the rounding gap against its theoretical bound.}
\end{enumerate}

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} positions the work against fluid-assisted and
learning-assisted scheduling. Section~\ref{sec:problem} states the problem, the fluid relaxation and the gap identity.
Section~\ref{sec:method} presents the method and its structural properties. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the
experiments and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
