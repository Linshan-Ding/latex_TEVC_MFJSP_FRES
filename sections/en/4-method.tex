\section{Fluid-residual evolutionary search}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overall framework}\label{sec:framework}
The method has four layers (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). The fluid layer solves \eqref{eq:fluid-obj}--\eqref{eq:fluid-dom}
once per instance and returns $L^F$, $q^*$ and $\lambda^*$. The quota layer rounds $q^*$ to the integer quota
$\mathbf{n}^0$ by the largest-remainder rule, builds the initial population and evaluates $g_Q$ and $g_S$ of every
individual through \eqref{eq:LQ}--\eqref{eq:decomp}. The candidate layer generates, for each parent, a pool $\mathcal{A}$ of
quota transfers and priority-key moves and takes the union of the top-$K$ sets of a quotient-graph ranker, a fluid heuristic
and a random draw. The verification layer decodes every candidate of the union exactly, selects the offspring by the true
makespan and applies elitist selection. The working hypothesis is that the fluid model already fixes the load and flow
structure, that integer quotas deviate from it only within a bounded rounding loss (Proposition~\ref{prop:rounding}), and
that the remaining freedom lies in the sequencing residual; a learner scoped to that residual faces a smaller and more
transferable task, while exact decoding and the safeguarded union cap the cost of a failing learner
(Proposition~\ref{prop:safeguard}).

\subsection{Quota-compressed representation and fluid--quota geometry}\label{sec:quota}
An individual is $\chi = (\mathbf{n}, \boldsymbol{\kappa})$: the integer quota tensor $\mathbf{n}$ of type-operations to
machines and one priority key $\kappa_{rk}$ per type-operation. No gene is kept per copy; copies are expanded at decoding
time, which removes the symmetry among identical copies.

\paragraph{Symmetry-aware event decoder} The decoder advances in time. When a machine $i$ becomes idle, it considers the
type-operations $(r,k)$ with $n_{rki} > 0$ that have at least one copy whose operation $k-1$ is complete, picks the one
with the best priority key, and among the eligible copies of that type picks the earliest releasable one. The start time
is the maximum of the copy's predecessor completion and the machine's ready time; the quota $n_{rki}$ is decremented.

\begin{theorem}[Decoder correctness]\label{thm:decoder}
Let $\mathbf{n}$ be a non-negative integer tensor with $n_{rki} = 0$ for $i \notin M_{rk}$ and $\sum_i n_{rki} = N_r$, and let
$\boldsymbol{\kappa}$ be arbitrary. Then (i) the event decoder terminates after exactly $\sum_r N_r K_r$ placements and
returns a schedule satisfying \eqref{eq:assign}--\eqref{eq:dom}; (ii) if $N_r = 1$ for all $r$, then $\mathbf{n}$ is a
machine-assignment vector, $\boldsymbol{\kappa}$ induces an operation permutation and the decoder is the active decoder of
the standard FJSP.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) A placement puts one copy-operation on machine $i$ only if $(r,k)$ still has quota on $i$ and some copy has its
predecessor complete; each placement decrements one quota and advances one copy by one operation. The quotas sum to
$\sum_r N_r K_r$, so the decoder stops after that many placements, and every copy-operation receives exactly one
candidate machine, which is \eqref{eq:assign}. The start time is at least the predecessor's completion, which is
\eqref{eq:prec}; operations on a machine are appended in placement order and do not overlap, so \eqref{eq:disj} holds with
$y$ equal to that order; \eqref{eq:cmax} and \eqref{eq:dom} hold by construction. A non-terminal state always admits a
placement: for every unfinished copy the first unfinished operation has its predecessor complete, and the remaining quota of
that type-operation equals the number of copies that have not yet performed it, so some machine holds a positive quota.
(ii) With $N_r = 1$, $n_{rki} \in \{0,1\}$ with exactly one unit per $(r,k)$, which is a machine-assignment vector; the
choice among copies is trivial, so the decoder selects among ready operations by priority key and starts each at the
earliest feasible time, which is active decoding.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Fluid--quota geometry} The fluid solution enters the search in two ways. At initialization the
largest-remainder rule maps $q^*$ to $\mathbf{n}^0$; during the search a quota transfer moves $b$ units of $n_{rk\cdot}$
from a machine whose quota exceeds $q^*_{rki}$ to a machine whose quota falls short of it, ties broken at random, so that
$\sum_i n_{rki} = N_r$ is conserved and $L^Q$ is updated incrementally through \eqref{eq:LQ}. Unlike fluid-assisted
evolutionary algorithms, the fluid solution defines the coordinate system of the representation and the direction of
the moves rather than an initialization; unlike the job-indexed encoding, the genotype dimension does not contain $N_r$.

\begin{proposition}[Rounding bound]\label{prop:rounding}
Let $q^*$ be optimal for \eqref{eq:fluid-obj}--\eqref{eq:fluid-dom}, let $\mathbf{n}^0$ round each $q^*_{rk\cdot}$ by the
largest-remainder rule, and let $B_i = \sum_{(r,k):\, i \in M_{rk}} p_{rki}$ and $\bar B = \max_i B_i$. Then
$0 \le L^Q(\mathbf{n}^0) - L^F \le \bar B$, and $\bar B$ does not depend on any $N_r$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound is Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp}. Largest-remainder rounding keeps $\sum_i n^0_{rki} = N_r$ and
$\lvert n^0_{rki} - q^*_{rki}\rvert < 1$. For machine $i$,
$\sum_{r,k} p_{rki} n^0_{rki} < \sum_{r,k} p_{rki} q^*_{rki} + \sum_{(r,k):\, i \in M_{rk}} p_{rki} \le L^F + B_i$ by
\eqref{eq:fluid-load}; also $\max_r \ell_r^{\min} \le L^F$ by \eqref{eq:fluid-dom}. Substituting into \eqref{eq:LQ}
gives $L^Q(\mathbf{n}^0) \le L^F + \bar B$. Each $B_i$ sums over type-operations only.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Quotient dimension]\label{prop:dims}
The quota chromosome has $\sum_r K_r \lvert M_{rk}\rvert + \sum_r K_r$ components; the quotient graph has
$\sum_r K_r + \lvert M\rvert$ nodes and $\sum_r (K_r - 1) + \sum_r K_r \lvert M_{rk}\rvert$ edges; none depends on $N_r$.
The job-indexed two-layer encoding has $2\sum_r N_r K_r$ components.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Count the definitions: one integer per $(r,k,i)$ with $i \in M_{rk}$ and one key per $(r,k)$; quotient nodes are
type-operations and machines, edges are consecutive type-operations of a type and type-operation--candidate pairs. The
job-indexed encoding keeps one machine gene and one sequence position per copy-operation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Residual-scoped quotient ranker}\label{sec:ranker}
The ranker works on the quotient graph of Proposition~\ref{prop:dims}. Type-operation nodes carry $N_r$, $K_r$,
$q^*_{rki}/N_r$, $n_{rki}/N_r$, the quota excess, processing-time statistics, current criticality and priority-key
statistics; machine nodes carry $\lambda^*_i$, the fluid and quota load shares and the idle and critical-load shares
observed in the decoder; global features are $g_Q$, $g_S$, $L^Q/L^F$, $\bar N$ and $V_N$. For a candidate action $a$ the
network forms a candidate embedding from the embeddings of the nodes it touches and the action type, outputs $\hat z_a$
and maps it through
\begin{equation}
\hat G_S(a) = L^F \operatorname{softplus}(\hat z_a), \label{eq:softplus}
\end{equation}
so that the predicted offspring makespan $L^Q(\mathbf{n}_a) + \hat G_S(a) \ge L^F$ never undercuts the analytic bound.
Labels $y_a = G_S(T_a\chi)/L^F$ are produced by exact decoding along the search trajectories of FRES-lite, the host without
the ranker, and the loss is pairwise logistic ranking within each parent. The ranker neither selects nor accepts actions
and needs no reinforcement-learning credit assignment; unlike a generic move ranker, its labels, features and output are
confined to the second term of \eqref{eq:decomp}, and the analytically known $L^Q(\mathbf{n}_a)$ is not learned.

\subsection{Safeguarded preselection}\label{sec:safeguard}
Each parent's pool $\mathcal{A}$ holds roughly equal numbers of quota transfers and priority-key moves (critical-block swaps,
critical-operation insertions and key perturbations). Only a small subset is decoded exactly:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eval}} = \operatorname{Top}_{K_L}\bigl(\hat G_S\bigr) \cup \operatorname{Top}_{K_H}(\text{fluid heuristic}) \cup \operatorname{Rand}_{K_R}, \label{eq:union}
\end{equation}
where the fluid heuristic scores a quota transfer by the reduction of $\sum_i \lvert n_{rki} - q^*_{rki}\rvert$ and a key
move by the critical-block membership of the operation it touches. The offspring is the candidate with the smallest decoded
makespan.

\begin{proposition}[Local non-inferiority of the safeguard]\label{prop:safeguard}
Let $\mathcal{A}_H \subseteq \mathcal{A}$ be the heuristic candidates and $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eval}} = \mathcal{A}_L \cup
\mathcal{A}_H \cup \mathcal{A}_R$, all decoded exactly. Then
$\min_{a \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eval}}} C_{\max}(T_a\chi) \le \min_{a \in \mathcal{A}_H} C_{\max}(T_a\chi)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathcal{A}_H \subseteq \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eval}}$ and a minimum is monotone under set inclusion. The statement does not
cover the ranker's inference cost; the wall-clock advantage is an experimental claim (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Algorithm, training and consistency}\label{sec:algorithm}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:fres} is the host search; Algorithm~\ref{alg:train} trains the ranker offline on random instances drawn
from the instance parameter table and freezes it, so that every evaluation instance is solved zero-shot.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{FRES: fluid-residual evolutionary search}\label{alg:fres}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require instance; population $\mu$, offspring $\lambda$; pool size $B$; $K_L, K_H, K_R$; quota step $b$; ranker $f$ (absent in FRES-lite); wall budget $T$
\Ensure best decoded schedule
\State solve \eqref{eq:fluid-obj}--\eqref{eq:fluid-dom}: $L^F, q^*, \lambda^*$; $\mathbf{n}^0 \gets$ largest-remainder rounding of $q^*$
\State $P \gets \mu$ individuals $(\mathbf{n}^0 \text{ with random transfers}, \boldsymbol{\kappa} \text{ random})$; decode each, record $C_{\max}, G_Q, G_S$
\While{elapsed time $< T$}
  \For{$t = 1$ \textbf{to} $\lambda$}
    \State $\chi \gets$ tournament parent from $P$
    \State $\mathcal{A} \gets$ pool of $B$ candidates: quota transfers (over-quota $\to$ under-quota machine, step $b$) and priority-key moves
    \State $\mathcal{A}_H \gets \operatorname{Top}_{K_H}$ of $\mathcal{A}$ by the fluid heuristic; $\mathcal{A}_L \gets \operatorname{Top}_{K_L}$ by $L^Q(\mathbf{n}_a) + f(a)$ if $f$ is given, else $\emptyset$; $\mathcal{A}_R \gets \operatorname{Rand}_{K_R}$ of the rest
    \ForAll{$a \in \mathcal{A}_L \cup \mathcal{A}_H \cup \mathcal{A}_R$}
      \State decode $T_a\chi$ exactly; record $C_{\max}$ and $G_S$ (and log $(\chi, a, G_S/L^F)$ when collecting labels)
    \EndFor
    \State add $\arg\min_a C_{\max}(T_a\chi)$ to the offspring set
  \EndFor
  \State $P \gets \mu+\lambda$ elitist selection by $C_{\max}$ with a diversity-rescue fraction
\EndWhile
\State \Return best individual of $P$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Offline training of the residual ranker}\label{alg:train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require instance parameter table; epochs $E$; instances per epoch $n_e$; learning rate; validation set
\Ensure frozen ranker $f$
\For{$e = 1$ \textbf{to} $E$}
  \State draw $n_e$ random instances; run Algorithm~\ref{alg:fres} without $f$ (FRES-lite) and log $(\chi, a, G_S(T_a\chi)/L^F)$ triples
  \State build the quotient graphs; update $f$ by pairwise logistic ranking loss over candidate pairs of the same parent
  \State evaluate full FRES with the current $f$ on the validation set; keep the best checkpoint; stop on plateau
\EndFor
\State \Return best checkpoint
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Consistency of the learning objective]\label{prop:consistency}
Let $\mathbf{n}_a$ be the quota of the offspring $T_a\chi$ (a transfer rewrites the quota deterministically and a key move
leaves it unchanged, so $\mathbf{n}_a$ is known without decoding). If $\hat G_S(a) = G_S(T_a\chi)$ for all $a \in \mathcal{A}$,
the ranking of candidates by $L^Q(\mathbf{n}_a) + \hat G_S(a)$ coincides with the ranking by the true $C_{\max}(T_a\chi)$, and
the pairwise ranking loss with labels $G_S(T_a\chi)/L^F$ is zero within a parent exactly when the prediction preserves the order
of $G_S$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp} applied to $T_a\chi$, $C_{\max}(T_a\chi) = L^F + G_Q(T_a\chi) + G_S(T_a\chi) =
L^Q(\mathbf{n}_a) + G_S(T_a\chi)$. With exact prediction the two keys agree term by term. Within a parent $L^F$ is a positive
constant, so dividing by it preserves the order, and a pairwise loss penalizes only inverted pairs.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Complexity} The fluid LP has $\sum_r K_r \lvert M_{rk}\rvert + 1$ variables and is solved once. A decoding costs
$O(\sum_r N_r K_r \log m)$ with a heap per machine; one generation costs $\lambda (K_L + K_H + K_R)$ decodings plus one
batched forward pass of the ranker over $\lambda B$ candidates, whose graph size is given by Proposition~\ref{prop:dims}.
The job-indexed host spends the same decodings on a genotype of size $2\sum_r N_r K_r$.

\subsection{Predictions tested in the experiments}\label{sec:predictions}
Performance statements are not theorems; they are stated as conjectures and attached to falsification conditions.

\begin{conjecture}[Sign of the rounding gap]\label{conj:rounding}
Under the instance distribution of Section~\ref{sec:settings}, the empirical relative rounding gap $G_Q(\mathbf{n}^0)/L^F$
decreases with $\bar N$ and increases with $V_p$.
\end{conjecture}
The bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:rounding} is $\bar B/L^F$; $L^F$ grows roughly linearly in $\bar N$ while $\bar B$ is
fixed, and larger $V_p$ separates the large terms of $B_i$ from the small ones that the fluid flow prefers. A non-monotone
curve in $\bar N$ or a reversed sign in $V_p$ refutes the conjecture.

\begin{conjecture}[Sign of the gain]\label{conj:gain}
On the main tier the gap improvement of FRES over the strongest baseline increases with $\bar N$ and $F$ and decreases
with $V_N$.
\end{conjecture}
Larger $\bar N$ raises the symmetry redundancy of the job-indexed representation while the quota representation is
unchanged (Proposition~\ref{prop:dims}); larger $F$ increases the quota freedom and the information in the fluid flow;
larger $V_N$ makes rare types round worse relative to their fluid mass. Any reversed sign refutes the conjecture and is
reported as such in Section~\ref{sec:factor}.
