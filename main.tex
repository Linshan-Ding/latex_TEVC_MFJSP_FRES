\begin{abstract}
Multiplicity flexible job-shop scheduling (MFJSP) describes shops in which a few job types are produced in many identical
copies, so that the search space grows with the number of copies while the copies themselves are interchangeable.
Existing fluid-assisted evolutionary and reinforcement-learning methods use the fluid relaxation as an initialization, a
state feature or a control signal, and do not say which part of the discrete difficulty the fluid model already explains.
This paper decomposes the gap between the fluid lower bound and a feasible makespan exactly into a rounding loss, incurred
when continuous quotas become integer quotas, and a sequencing residual under fixed integer quotas. A quota chromosome whose
dimension does not grow with the number of copies, together with conservation-preserving quota transfers, carries the first
term; a quotient-graph ranker that predicts only the sequencing residual, with an output that never violates the analytic
lower bound, carries the second, and a safeguarded preselection that unites learned, fluid-heuristic and random candidates
keeps every decision under exact decoding. \pending{On 59 fixed design instances crossing three structural factors, the
Brandimarte benchmark and a multiplicity-extrapolation tier, the method reaches makespan gaps no worse than job-indexed
genetic search, fluid-assisted evolutionary search and two learning-based baselines under the same wall-clock budget, with
\OursDecodeReduction{} fewer exact decodings.} \pending{The empirical rounding gap decreases with the mean multiplicity as the
theory predicts.}
\end{abstract}
